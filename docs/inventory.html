Part 1: Inventory & Pharmacy Operations — Complete Overview

1. Purchase Orders (PO) & Procurement

Initiation

  • Indent/Request: Departments (wards, OT, ICU) raise material indents when stock reaches reorder levels. The pharmacy/inventory department consolidates these.
  • Rate Contracts: Many hospitals have annual rate contracts with vendors for commonly used items (especially generics and consumables), negotiated by the purchase committee.
  • Tender Process: For high-value or bulk purchases, hospitals may float tenders. For mid-sized hospitals, this is often done quarterly or annually.
  • Emergency Purchases: Local purchases are authorized for urgent needs, often with a spending limit (e.g., ₹10,000–₹50,000) requiring approval from the medical superintendent or administrator.

PO Creation

  • POs are generated in the Hospital Information System (HIS) or ERP module.
  • Details include: item name, brand/generic name, quantity, rate, vendor, expected delivery date, terms (credit period, typically 30–60 days).
  • Approval Workflow: Pharmacy head → Finance/Accounts → Hospital Administrator (depending on amount).
  • GST Compliance: POs specify HSN codes and GST rates (5%, 12%, or 18% for medicines; 5% or 12% for medical devices).

2. Vendor Invoices & Goods Receipt

Goods Receipt Note (GRN)

When stock arrives, the store/pharmacy officer verifies:

  • Physical quantity vs. PO quantity
  • Batch numbers, manufacturing dates (MFG), expiry dates
  • Cold chain integrity (for vaccines, biologics, insulin)
  • Packaging integrity and damage
Shortages/Damages: Recorded on the delivery challan; goods returned or credit notes requested.

GRN Entry: Entered in the system against the PO. This updates "Goods Received but Invoice Not Received" (GRIN) status.

Invoice Processing

  • Vendor submits invoice (often after delivery or with the delivery).
  • Three-way matching: PO → GRN → Invoice. The accounts department verifies:
    • Rates match the PO/contract
    • Quantities match GRN
    • GST details are correct
    • Valid e-way bill (if applicable)
  • Payment: After verification, processed as per credit terms. Many hospitals pay vendors on a 30–45 day cycle.

3. Stock Entry & Inventory Management

Categorization & Storage

  • Drug Classification: Stored by category—antibiotics, cardiac, analgesics, surgical consumables, etc.
  • Storage Conditions:
    • Room temperature (15–25°C)
    • Cold chain (2–8°C) for vaccines, insulin, certain biologics
    • Controlled substances (Schedule H, H1, X) in locked cabinets with restricted access
  • FEFO (First Expiry First Out): Strictly followed. New stock placed behind old stock.

Stock Entry in System

After GRN, stock is "accepted" into inventory:

  • Batch-wise entry (critical for traceability)
  • MFG date, expiry date, purchase rate, MRP
  • Location mapping (rack/bin number in the store)
  • Opening Stock: Some items (OTC, consumables) may also be received as opening stock when a new pharmacy counter is set up.

Inventory Controls

Control Description
Reorder Levels (ROL) System alerts when stock hits the minimum level (e.g., 15 days of consumption)
ABC Analysis A items: High-value, low-volume (strict control, frequent review)
B items: Moderate value
C items: Low-value, high-volume (OTC, consumables)
Expiry Management Monthly expiry reports generated; near-expiry items (3–6 months) are flagged for return to vendor or discounted disposal

4. Pharmacy Sales — OTC (Outpatient / Walk-in)

Process Flow

  1. Prescription or Direct Request: Patient presents a prescription or asks for OTC items (e.g., paracetamol, antacids, bandages).
  2. Billing & Dispensing:
    • Pharmacist enters items in the POS/billing module.
    • System checks stock availability (batch-wise).
    • MRP is auto-populated; any discount (if hospital policy allows) is applied.
    • GST is calculated (usually included in MRP for medicines).
  3. Payment: Cash, UPI, card, or wallet. Some hospitals allow credit for staff or corporate tie-ups.
  4. Dispensing:
    • Label printing: dosage instructions, patient name, expiry date.
    • FEFO compliance: oldest batch dispensed first.
    • Counseling: Pharmacist explains dosage, side effects, food interactions (especially for antibiotics, diabetes meds).
  5. Stock Deduction: Real-time reduction in inventory. Batch traceability maintained.
Key Regulatory Points:
• OTC Scope: In India, Schedule H and H1 drugs require prescriptions. OTC sales are limited to non-scheduled items.
• Narcotics/Controlled Drugs: Schedule X drugs require special registers, double-lock storage, and detailed dispensing logs (patient name, doctor, quantity, balance).

5. Pharmacy for In-Patients (IP Pharmacy / Ward Supply)

Indent-Based System (Most Common)

  1. Doctor's Order: Physician enters medication orders in the HIS (CPOE — Computerized Physician Order Entry).
  2. Pharmacy Requisition (Indent): The system or ward nurse generates an indent for the required medicines/consumables.
  3. Pharmacy Processing:
    • IP pharmacy receives the indent.
    • Picks items batch-wise from inventory.
    • Prepares doses (unit-dose packaging for some hospitals).
  4. Dispatch to Ward: Delivered via pneumatic chutes, trolleys, or ward runners.
  5. Administration: Nurse administers and documents in the e-MAR (electronic Medication Administration Record).
  6. Billing: Automatically posted to the patient's bill in real-time or at discharge (depending on HIS configuration).

Floor Stock / Ward Stock Model

  • High-use items (IV fluids, syringes, cotton, common antibiotics) are kept as floor stock in wards.
  • Nurses consume and document usage; pharmacy replenishes periodically (daily or based on PAR levels).
  • Reconciliation: Periodic physical stock checks in wards vs. system consumption.

Discharge Medication (TTO — To Take Out)

  • When a patient is discharged, the doctor prescribes take-home medication.
  • Pharmacy dispenses against the discharge summary; billed under the final IP bill or separately at the pharmacy counter.
  • Often includes 3–7 days of antibiotics, painkillers, or chronic disease medication.

6. Returns, Wastage & Disposal

Type Process
Vendor Returns Near-expiry or excess stock returned to vendors as per agreement (usually before 3–6 months of expiry)
Patient Returns Unopened, non-damaged medicines may be returned in some hospitals (policy-dependent; rare in India)
Ward Returns Unused indents returned to pharmacy; stock re-entered if intact
Wastage & Disposal Expired stock, broken vials, or contaminated items segregated as biomedical waste per BMW Rules 2016; pharmaceutical waste disposed via authorized agencies
Write-off Approved by hospital administration; recorded in inventory system

7. Key Systems & Compliance

Software Systems

  • HIS/ERP Modules: Common ones in India include Medanta, Fortis (in-house), Practo, eHospital (NIC), HIS by Tata Technologies, SoftClinic, MediSteer, or custom-built ERPs.
  • Integration: Pharmacy module linked to billing, IP management, lab, and accounts.

Regulatory Compliance

Regulation Requirement
Drugs & Cosmetics Act, 1940 Licensing (retail drug license from State Drug Control Department)
Schedule H/H1/X Strict documentation for prescription-only and narcotic drugs
GST Monthly returns; input tax credit on purchases
NABH Accreditation Traceability, FEFO, expiry management, patient counseling protocols
Biomedical Waste Management Rules Proper segregation and disposal

8. Typical Pain Points in Mid-Sized Indian Hospitals

Issue Why It Happens
Stock-outs of critical drugs Poor demand forecasting, delayed PO approvals
Expiry losses Over-ordering, poor FEFO adherence, lack of batch tracking
Billing leakage Manual IP indenting, ward floor stock not reconciled
Vendor payment delays Three-way matching bottlenecks, disputed invoices
Narcotics tracking gaps Manual registers, lack of real-time system integration
GST mismatches Incorrect HSN codes, invoice-PO mismatches

Part 2: Consumables in Hospital Software — Complete Treatment Guide

1. What Counts as "Consumables" in a Hospital

Category Examples Where Used
Surgical consumables Sutures, gloves, drapes, catheters, syringes, needles OT, procedure rooms, wards
Medical consumables IV sets, cannulas, oxygen masks, nebulizer kits, Foley's IP wards, ICU, emergency
General consumables Cotton, gauze, bandages, surgical tape All departments
Diagnostic consumables Blood collection tubes, reagents, strips Lab, radiology
Patient amenities Toothbrush, soap, bed sheets (disposable) IP billing (often package)

2. Master Data Setup in Software

Item Master Classification

Most HIS/ERP systems classify consumables under non-drug inventory or surgical stores. Key fields:

Item Code: SUR-GLV-001 Item Name: Surgical Gloves (Sterile, Size 7.5) Category: Surgical Consumables Sub-category: Hand Protection UOM: Pair (or Box of 50 pairs) Storage: Surgical Store / OT Store Valuation: FIFO (or Weighted Average) GST HSN: 4015 11 00 (18% or 12% depending) Re-order Level: 100 boxes Re-order Qty: 500 boxes Vendor Preference: Vendor A (Rate Contract) Is Billable to Patient: Yes / No Is Part of Package: Yes / No Charge Code: OT-CHG-045 (links to billing master)

Critical Decision: Billable vs. Non-Billable

Type How Treated in Software Example
Billable consumables Linked to a charge master code; billed per use or per procedure Sutures, stents, implants, special gloves
Non-billable (absorbed) Cost absorbed into room rent or OT charges; tracked for cost analysis but not separately billed Standard gauze, cotton, routine syringes
Package-linked Pre-defined quantity included in surgery package; excess billed separately Knee replacement package includes 2 packs sutures; 3rd pack is extra

3. Procurement & Stock Entry

Purchase Order

  • Consumables often have annual rate contracts with surgical supply vendors (e.g., Ethicon, Romsons, Polymed).
  • POs may specify: brand, size, sterilization status (gamma/EO), lot number traceability (critical for implants).

Goods Receipt (GRN)

  • Batch/lot tracking mandatory for surgical items (recall management).
  • Sterility check: Expiry of sterilization (not just manufacturing date).
  • Implant tracking: Unique serial number entry for each implant (stent, pacemaker, orthopedic implant) — required for NABH and medico-legal.

Stock Entry Nuances

GRN Entry: - Item: Suture (Vicryl 2-0, CT-1 needle) - Batch: VIC2026A - MFG: Jan 2026 - Expiry: Dec 2028 - Sterility Expiry: Dec 2028 (if applicable) - Serial Numbers: [for implants only] - Location: OT Store, Rack B-12 - Qty Received: 50 boxes - Unit Cost: ₹450 - MRP: ₹650

4. How Consumables Flow to Patient Billing

Method A: Indent-Based (Ward/OT → Pharmacy/Store)

Step 1: Doctor orders procedure (e.g., central line insertion) Step 2: Nurse raises indent in HIS for: - Central Venous Catheter Kit (1) - Surgical gloves (2 pairs) - Syringe 10ml (2) - Dressing kit (1) Step 3: Store dispatches against indent Step 4: System auto-posts to patient bill: - CVC Kit: ₹2,400 - Gloves: ₹180 (₹90 x 2) - Syringes: ₹40 (₹20 x 2) - Dressing: ₹150 [Total: ₹2,770 added to IP bill] Step 5: Nurse documents actual usage in e-MAR/care plan
Software Logic: The indent "reserves" stock; actual consumption is confirmed by nurse documentation. Some systems auto-consume on indent; others require explicit "administer/used" confirmation.

Method B: Procedure/Surgery Template (OT Charges)

Surgery Master: "Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy" Linked Consumables: - Port (10mm): 3 units → ₹4,500 - Port (5mm): 2 units → ₹2,000 - Endo GIA stapler: 1 unit → ₹8,500 - Trocar: 3 units → ₹3,600 - Clip applier: 1 unit → ₹2,200 - Standard consumables (absorbed): gloves, gauze, sutures When surgery is scheduled: → System auto-generates OT consumables indent → Surgeon can modify quantities intra-op → Excess usage flagged for billing approval → Final bill = package charge + extra consumables

Method C: Floor Stock with Periodic Reconciliation

Ward Floor Stock (Daily): - IV Cannula 18G: 50 units - Syringes 5ml: 100 units - Normal Saline 500ml: 40 bottles Daily/Shift: - Nurse documents usage per patient in HIS - System auto-deducts from floor stock + posts to patient bill - Pharmacy replenishes to PAR level At month-end: - Physical stock count vs. system consumption - Variance report → adjustment/write-off

5. Special Cases & Software Handling

Implants & High-Value Items

  • Unique serialization: Each implant tracked by serial number from GRN to patient.
  • Patient linkage: In software, implant S/N linked to patient UHID and surgery record.
  • Implant card: Auto-generated and given to patient (mandatory for cardiac stents, orthopedic implants).
  • Return policy: Non-returnable once opened; tracked for vendor warranty.

Reusable vs. Disposable

Type Software Treatment
Disposable Standard inventory, auto-deduct on use
Reusable (e.g., certain scopes, instruments) Asset register, not inventory; track sterilization cycles
Semi-reusable (e.g., diathermy pencils) Sometimes tracked as "patient-specific" single-use

Non-Billable / Absorbed Consumables

  • Still tracked in inventory for costing and profitability analysis.
  • Software reports: "Cost of consumables per surgery," "Ward consumption vs. occupancy."
  • Helps administrators decide when to revise package pricing.

7. Common Software Pitfalls & How to Avoid Them

Problem Root Cause Software Fix
Patient billed for consumables not used Nurse indent ≠ actual usage Require "confirm usage" step; don't auto-bill on indent alone
OT consumables not captured Surgeon uses item not in template Mobile/tablet entry intra-op; "add unplanned item" workflow
Implants not traceable Serial number not entered at GRN Mandatory serial field; block GRN completion without it
Floor stock leakage No periodic reconciliation Daily shift-end stock count prompt in system
GST mismatch on consumables Wrong HSN code in master HSN validation at master creation; periodic audit
Package profitability unknown Absorbed consumables not costed Map absorbed items to surgery; run cost reports

Key Design Decision for Software Architects:

Consumables can be treated as either:

1. Inventory items that happen to be billable (inventory-centric)
2. Charge items that happen to be stocked (billing-centric)

Most mid-sized Indian hospitals end up with a hybrid approach — implants and high-value items are inventory-tracked; routine consumables are often "absorbed" or managed as floor stock with loose tracking.
